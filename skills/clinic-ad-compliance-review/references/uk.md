# United Kingdom: health advertising rules for clinics

Reference file of the skill `clinic-ad-compliance-review`. Read it only for a clinic in the UK, after the SKILL.md. Certainty is high unless marked medium; the phrases to flag are in the blocks under each section, and the source codes are listed at the end with their full URLs.

Rules checked: November 2026

## 1. Who regulates whom

- **Chiropractors:** the General Chiropractic Council (GCC). «Chiropractor» is a protected title. Its Code of Professional Practice is in force since 1 January 2026; advertising is standard C5 [UKP1].
- **Osteopaths:** the General Osteopathic Council (GOsC) [UKP10]. **Physiotherapists:** the HCPC [UKP12]. **Dentists:** the GDC [UKP13]. Some aesthetic practitioners have no statutory regulator.
- **Ads, landing pages and organic posts** follow the CAP Code, applied by the ASA. Since 7 April 2025 its rule numbers changed: «free» is 3.23 to 3.26, reviews and testimonials 3.44 to 3.50, urgency 3.30 to 3.31 [UKA1]. The DMCC Act 2024 bans some practices outright [UKL1].
- **The clinic stays accountable for copy an agency writes** (GCC C5: «Where you delegate this, the accountability sits with you»), and the professional standards cover organic posts and blogs too [UKP1].

## 2. Rules for every clinic

| # | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| U1 | Hold documentary evidence before publishing any objective claim; health claims need human studies. Anything outside the ASA lists in section 3 counts as unsupported unless the clinic has that evidence. | UKA1 r.3.7; UKA3 r.12.1; UKA23 |
| U2 | No misleading claim, exaggeration or overall impression, even when each statement is true: assess the whole ad, images included. | UKA1 rr.3.1, 3.11; UKL3 |
| U3 | No «cure», no guarantees and no safety claims without proof; falsely claiming a service can cure illnesses or restore function is a banned practice. Rewrite as «may help relieve…» for accepted conditions only. | UKA3 rr.12.6, 12.7, 12.9; UKL1 Sch 20 para 19 |
| U4 | «Natural» doesn't mean safe or effective. | UKA3 r.12.10 |
| U5 | Nothing that discourages essential treatment for conditions that need medical supervision, and no anti-vaccine content. | UKA3 r.12.2 |
| U6 | No fear or shock without justification, and no exploiting a lack of health knowledge. Personal-attribute wording («Do you have back pain?») is Meta's policy, not a UK legal rule; these two are the nearest UK rules. | UKA5 r.4.2; UKP1 C5 |
| U7 | No fake reviews; incentives (discounts, free sessions, vouchers) disclosed; no asking only happy patients, no hiding negative reviews, no extra prominence for positive ones. A clinic that publishes reviews takes reasonable steps against fakes. | UKL1 Sch 20 para 13; UKL9; UKA1 rr.3.44 to 3.46 |
| U8 | Testimonials are genuine, documented, about the service and used with permission; claims inside them need the same evidence, because a testimonial isn't evidence. Flag testimonials that name conditions outside section 3; rewrite them around the experience («felt listened to») or remove them. | UKA1 rr.3.47 to 3.50; UKA14 |
| U9 | Before-and-after images count as testimonials: signed, dated proof they're genuine and unaltered, and evidence the result is typical; a disclaimer doesn't fix a misleading image (medium for posture photos and X-ray comparisons). | UKA15 |
| U10 | «Free» only if the consumer pays nothing beyond the unavoidable cost of responding; «complimentary» counts; a part of a package can't be called free. Otherwise state the conditions next to the claim. | UKA1 rr.3.23 to 3.26; UKA16; UKL1 Sch 20 para 23 |
| U11 | A headline price includes mandatory fees; if the offer depends on joining a plan, say so; give the clinic's identity. No drip pricing. | UKL2; UKA1 rr.3.18, 3.21, 3.22 |
| U12 | No false urgency or scarcity; promotions show a prominent closing date and significant conditions. | UKL1 Sch 20 para 7; UKA1 rr.3.30, 3.31; UKA4 r.8.17 |
| U13 | Unqualified superlatives («best», «No.1», «leading») are comparative claims that need evidence. | UKA1 rr.3.32 to 3.41 |
| U14 | No fake trust marks: a GCC, NHS or professional-body logo only if authorised. | UKA1 rr.3.52, 3.53 |

## 3. Conditions each profession may name (ASA)

**Chiropractors** may name only these conditions (ASA, 1 June 2023); images and surrounding text mustn't stretch past them [UKA7]:

- ankle sprain (short-term management); cramp; elbow pain and tennis elbow arising from associated musculoskeletal conditions of the back and neck, but not isolated occurrences;
- headache arising from the neck (cervicogenic); joint pains; hip and knee pain from osteoarthritis, as an adjunct to core treatments and exercise;
- general, acute and chronic backache and back pain (not arising from injury or accident); generalised aches and pains; lumbago;
- mechanical neck pain (as opposed to neck pain following injury, i.e. whiplash); migraine prevention; minor sports injuries; muscle spasms;
- plantar fasciitis (short-term management); rotator cuff injuries, disease or disorders; sciatica (general references, not pain-reduction claims);
- shoulder complaints (dysfunction, disorders and pain, but not frozen shoulder); soft tissue disorders of the shoulder; tension and inability to relax.

**Excluded for chiropractors** [UKA7][UKA8]: whiplash, or neck or back pain caused by an injury or accident; frozen shoulder; an unqualified «we treat sports injuries»; claims that chiropractic eliminates sciatic pain; colic, crying babies, birth trauma, breech, morning sickness, feeding problems, plagiocephaly, shorter labour; «many midwives refer»; «full breadth of primary care».

ASA examples of acceptable wording [UKA8]: «Conditions we treat: Sciatica»; «Chiropractic can help with neck pain caused by poor posture or awkward sitting positions»; «We treat minor sports injuries such as [an accepted condition]». Context can make an accepted claim unacceptable.

**Osteopaths** (ASA, 1 November 2022) [UKA11]: arthritic pain; circulatory problems; cramp; digestion problems; fibromyalgia; frozen shoulder, shoulder and elbow pain, tennis elbow (arising from associated musculoskeletal conditions of the back and neck, not isolated occurrences); headache arising from the neck; joint pains, including hip and knee pain from osteoarthritis as an adjunct to core treatments and exercise; general, acute and chronic backache and back pain (not from injury or accident); generalised aches and pains; lumbago; migraine prevention; minor sports injuries; muscle spasms; neuralgia; tension and inability to relax; rheumatic pain; sciatica; uncomplicated mechanical neck pain (not after injury, i.e. whiplash). Conditions specific to babies, children or pregnancy (colic, reflux, glue ear, plagiocephaly, sleeping or feeding problems and others) are unlikely to be acceptable.

**Physiotherapists** (ASA, 20 February 2024) [UKA12]: as statutory regulated professionals, likely accepted for general aches and pains, arthritic pain, backache, back pain, circulatory problems, cramp, digestion problems, joint pains, lumbago, muscle spasms, neuralgia, fibromyalgia, minor sports injuries and tensions; other conditions need documentary evidence, most likely clinical trials.

## 4. Titles, registration and chiropractic claims

| # | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| U15 | A chiropractor's «Dr» is qualified straight away, in the same statement: «Doctor of Chiropractic», a courtesy title, no general medical qualification. «DC» alone isn't enough; only GCC-registered chiropractors may use it. | UKA9; UKA10 |
| U16 | Osteopaths without a general medical qualification avoid «Dr»; dentists don't imply a medical qualification. | UKA11; UKA20; UKA22 |
| U17 | Patients are told the chiropractor is GCC-registered: add the registration line or mark (improvement if missing from an ad). | UKP1 C3 |
| U18 | No subluxation as a cause of disease, «innate intelligence», «everyone needs a monthly adjustment» or «prevents illness». | UKP3; UKP7 |

Pattern for U15: «Dr [name], Doctor of Chiropractic. "Dr" is a courtesy title; [name] is not a medical doctor.»

## 5. Dental and aesthetics

- **Dental** [UKP13; UKA20; UKA21]: no unjustified expectations; on deal sites, say the treatment may not suit every patient and depends on an assessment; show the GDC number, qualification and NHS or private status; «specialist» only on a GDC specialist list. The GDC consulted in 2026 on replacing these standards (pending) [UKP14].
- **Aesthetics** [UKA19; UKA18]: no time-pressure offers at all (no countdowns), no trivialising procedures, no exploiting insecurities, no targeting under-18s. Prescription-only medicines can't be advertised to the public: Botox can't be named in ads, hashtags or price lists, and «anti-wrinkle injections» can be an indirect ad; use «consultation for the treatment of lines and wrinkles».

## 6. Not verified

- The CAP Code shows no edition date; changes after 7 April 2025 weren't checked.
- No 2026 edition of GCC advertising guidance was found; the 2021 guidance is probably legacy. No ASA ruling on chiropractic care plans was found.
- The GOsC practice standards are under review; Chartered Society of Physiotherapy guidance wasn't read.
- Fine amounts aren't stated here: don't give any.
