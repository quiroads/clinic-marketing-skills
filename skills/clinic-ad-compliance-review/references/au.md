# Australia: health advertising rules for clinics

Reference file of the skill `clinic-ad-compliance-review`. Read it only for a clinic in Australia, after the SKILL.md. Certainty is high unless marked medium; the phrases to flag are in the blocks under each section, and the source codes are listed at the end with their full URLs.

Rules checked: November 2026

## 1. The five advertising offences (National Law s.133)

Anyone advertising a regulated health service (chiropractic, physiotherapy, osteopathy, dental, medical and the other registered professions) must not advertise in a way that [AU1 s.133(1)]:

- (a) is false, misleading or deceptive, or likely to be;
- (b) offers a gift, discount or other inducement without stating its terms and conditions;
- (c) uses testimonials or purported testimonials about the service;
- (d) creates an unreasonable expectation of beneficial treatment;
- (e) directly or indirectly encourages the indiscriminate or unnecessary use of health services.

It applies to practitioners, people who aren't registered and companies. Whoever controls or authorises the content is the advertiser, so the clinic answers for copy its staff or agency drafts [AU2 s.3.2]. Each offence carries its own penalty under s.133: don't state amounts (the guidelines' penalty section is out of date).

## 2. Rules

| # | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | A testimonial is any positive statement about the clinical aspects of a service: a symptom, diagnosis, treatment or outcome. It can't be used in advertising, even if genuine, and that includes reviews the clinic republishes, shares, embeds or boosts, and patient stories. Comments only about customer service or communication aren't testimonials. The clinic answers for reviews on its own website and social media (turning the reviews function off is one way); it isn't responsible for reviews on platforms it doesn't control, but engaging with them may count as using a testimonial. | AU2 s.4.3; AU4; AU5 |
| A2 | Offers state their terms and conditions (who qualifies, what's included, expiry, total price), in the ad or easily found from it. «Free» means absolutely free: not recovered through a price rise or through other sources such as Medicare. | AU1 s.133(1)(b); AU2 s.4.2 |
| A3 | No urgency or fear tied to health («don't delay», «act now before it's too late», «for a limited time only» linked to health suffering); no implying that health will suffer without the service, unless evidence supports it; no promoting regular or periodic appointments without clinical need, contracting for future services included. | AU2 ss.4.4, 4.5 |
| A4 | Effectiveness claims need acceptable evidence, mostly peer-reviewed research; anecdotes and uncontrolled before-and-after studies don't count. Listing health conditions a treatment can help, without qualification, may mislead. «Safe», «effective», «risk-free» or «pain-free» can create unreasonable expectations unless risks are acknowledged. | AU2 s.4.1, s.4.4 |
| A5 | A practitioner who isn't a medical doctor and uses «Dr» names the profession: «Dr [name] (Chiropractor)». «Specialist», «specialises in» or «specialised» only with specialist registration (chiropractors have none; dental specialists only if registered as such); «has a specific interest in» is fine. | AU2 s.4.1.4; AU5 |
| A6 | Before-and-after images only if genuine and comparable (same angle, lighting, posture, clothing), with any alteration explained and only the treated change shown. | AU2 s.4.4.1 |
| A7 | Higher-risk non-surgical cosmetic procedures (injectables, fillers, thread lifts, dental veneers): no targeting under-18s, no trivialising terms («doll-maker», «world's best»), no sexualised images; the advertiser answers for influencer content. TGA rules on naming injectable brands aren't covered. | AU6 |
| A8 | Australian Consumer Law: no misleading or deceptive conduct, no false testimonials; no fake reviews, no suppressing or editing negative ones; any review incentive applies whatever the rating and is disclosed. | AU7 s.18, s.29(1)(e)(f); AU8 |

<!-- flag-examples:start -->
- A1: «Finally sciatica-free after two visits!», «5 stars: my headaches are gone»
- A2: «free spinal check», «$49 first visit», «2-for-1»
- A3: «Book before your back gets worse!», «Lock in 24 visits and save 30%»
- A4: «safe and effective», «pain-free»
- A5: «Dr Lee», «spine specialist», «specialises in sports injuries»
<!-- flag-examples:end -->

A1 also covers a patient success story and a star-rating carousel with clinical comments. A2's phrases break the rule when X-rays are charged or the terms are missing; A4 also covers an unqualified list of conditions the service «can help»; A5's «Dr» when the profession isn't named next to it.

## 3. Chiropractic: what Ahpra's examples allow

- **Allowed:** «may help patients to manage a range of musculoskeletal conditions»; naming back pain, neck pain and joint stiffness; managing symptoms such as muscular tension often associated with asthma; «chiropractors are spine experts» [AU5].
- **Not allowed, and can't be fixed by rewording:** claims about asthma, indigestion, ear infections, behaviour, immunity, fertility, foetal position or children's development; ADHD, autism, colic or bedwetting; regular spinal checks of babies to prevent illness; «spine specialists» [AU5].
- **Children:** the Chiropractic Board's statement on paediatric care (in force 9 September 2026) strongly advises against spinal manipulation for children under two: never advertise it [AU9].

## 4. Other professions

Physiotherapy, osteopathy and dental clinics follow the same s.133 offences and Ahpra guidelines. Their boards' profession-specific statements weren't checked: say so when one could apply. Aesthetics and veneers: A7.

## 5. Safer patterns

| Type | Safer pattern |
|---|---|
| Testimonial or clinical review | Remove it; keep non-clinical comments only («friendly team, easy booking»). |
| Condition list | «Our chiropractors may help you manage a range of musculoskeletal conditions, such as back and neck pain.» |
| Offer | «[offer], [price]. T&Cs: [who qualifies], [what's included], [expiry]. No obligation to start care.» |
| Title | «Dr [name] (Chiropractor)». |
