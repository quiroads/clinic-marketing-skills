# United States: health advertising rules for clinics

Reference file of the skill `clinic-ad-compliance-review`. Read it only for a clinic in the US, after the SKILL.md. Federal rules apply everywhere; the state rules below are cumulative. Certainty is high unless marked medium; the phrases to flag are in the blocks under each section, and the source codes are listed at the end with their full URLs.

Rules checked: November 2026

## 1. Federal rules (FTC, Medicare)

| # | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | A health benefit claim for a service needs competent and reliable scientific evidence, generally randomized controlled human trials, before the ad runs. Patient experiences and the practitioner's own observations are not substantiation. High on the statute (it covers «services»); medium on applying the FTC's product guidance to services. Ask for the study behind each efficacy claim; if there is none, describe the service without a promised outcome. | US1; US2 |
| F2 | A testimonial about results is read as the typical result. If it isn't typical, disclose clearly what patients generally get; «results not typical» alone doesn't meet the rule (inference from the text). A health testimonial needs the same evidence as if the clinic made the claim. | US3 s.255.2(b) |
| F3 | Disclose material connections behind an endorsement: staff, family, paid influencers, or patients who got a free or discounted visit. | US3 s.255.5 |
| F4 | No fake reviews, no incentives tied to a positive rating, no undisclosed reviews by managers or staff, no hiding negative reviews; a review section mustn't suggest it shows all reviews when some are suppressed. Asking every patient for a review is allowed. | US4; US3 s.255.2(d) |
| F5 | «Free» must be free: all conditions stated clearly at the outset, and the cost not recovered by raising the price of what the patient must buy next. One kind of service shouldn't carry a «free» offer for more than six months in a year in one area. | US5 |
| F6 | Free or discounted services offered to Medicare or Medicaid patients can be an illegal inducement; only items of nominal value as the OIG defines it, never cash (medium). If the clinic bills Medicare or Medicaid, the offer must exclude those patients or be checked by a healthcare attorney. | US6 |

<!-- flag-examples:start -->
- F1: «fixes sciatica», «cures headaches», «boosts immunity»
- F2: «results not typical»
- F4: «happy with your visit? Leave us a review»
<!-- flag-examples:end -->

F2 also covers a patient quote with a result and nothing about typical results; «results not typical» alone isn't the fix. F4's request breaks the rule when it goes only to satisfied patients.

## 2. Texas

| # | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | Health-care advertising may not contain a testimonial, create an unjustified expectation about results, make unsubstantiated comparisons, or play on the fears or emotions of susceptible patients. Remove patient testimonials from ads and landing pages, review widgets used as advertising included. High on the text; medium on which professions it covers. | US7 s.101.201(b) |
| T2 | Chiropractors: a «free» service lists all the component services it includes; research-based claims identify the study; no guarantees, cures, claims outside chiropractic scope, results beyond scientific proof, or a false expectation of harm from not being treated (medium: text read through a reproduction). | US8 22 TAC 77.1, 77.4 |

<!-- flag-examples:start -->
- T1: «Don't wait until it's permanent»
<!-- flag-examples:end -->

T1 also covers any patient testimonial.

## 3. Florida

| # | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| FL1 | Any ad for a free, discounted-fee or reduced-fee service, examination or treatment by a licensee under the chapters s.456.062 lists (chapter 460, chiropractic medicine, among them; check the others for other professions) carries this statement in capital letters, clearly distinguishable from the rest of the text. On radio or TV it is read aloud or shown for at least 15 seconds. Copy it exactly from below the table. | US9; US10 |
| FL2 | Every chiropractic ad names the chiropractor and their degree. A «free x-ray» ad also says x-rays are given only if medically necessary. | US10 |
| FL3 | Chiropractors: no false or unjustified expectations of beneficial treatment or cures, no claims about the quality of the services, no claim to be better than other practitioners beyond listing earned credentials; no «M.D.» or «D.O.» without that licence. | US10 64B2-15.001(2) |

The FL1 statement, word for word:

```text
THE PATIENT AND ANY OTHER PERSON RESPONSIBLE FOR PAYMENT HAS A RIGHT TO REFUSE TO PAY, CANCEL PAYMENT, OR BE REIMBURSED FOR PAYMENT FOR ANY OTHER SERVICE, EXAMINATION, OR TREATMENT THAT IS PERFORMED AS A RESULT OF AND WITHIN 72 HOURS OF RESPONDING TO THE ADVERTISEMENT FOR THE FREE, DISCOUNTED FEE, OR REDUCED FEE SERVICE, EXAMINATION, OR TREATMENT.
```

- FL3: «best chiropractor in Miami», «top-rated care», «the most advanced technique»
<!-- flag-examples:end -->

## 4. California

| # | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| CA1 | Chiropractors: no distorted, sensational or fabulous statements. | US11 16 CCR 311 |
| CA2 | A free or discounted service: no other charge in that visit unless the patient was told the cost first and agreed; the evaluation of a free test isn't billed separately. | US11 16 CCR 319 |
| CA3 | Healing-arts ads: before-and-after images say which procedures were done, are comparable in presentation and say the same results may not occur for all patients; model images are labelled; superiority claims need objective scientific evidence, and scientific claims peer-reviewed support. High on the text; medium on its reach to chiropractors. | US12 B&P 651(b) |

## 5. New York

| # | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| NY1 | Testimonials only with the patient's express written authorization, identity disclosures and reasonable disclaimers; no fictional testimonials. No guarantee of any service; no unsubstantiated or superiority claims; no bonus or inducement other than a discount; a fixed price states the period it applies to and any integral extra charges. Keep an exact copy of each ad for one year. | US13 8 NYCRR 29.1(b) |

<!-- flag-examples:start -->
- NY1: «free gift with your first visit»
<!-- flag-examples:end -->

## 6. Other states and other professions

- **Other states** aren't covered: apply the federal rules and say the state wasn't checked.
- **Professions:** Texas s.101.201 reaches health-care professionals generally (medium); New York 29.1 covers every licensed Title VIII profession (chiropractic, physical therapy, dentistry and others); Florida s.456.062 covers the chapters it lists; California B&P 651 covers healing-arts licensees.

## 7. Safer patterns

| Type | Safer pattern |
|---|---|
| Condition question | «Back pain and sciatica care in [city]: what your first visit includes.» |
| Efficacy claim | «We assess your spine and explain your options, including [therapies offered].» |
| Free exam, Florida | The offer, its conditions, «X-rays only if medically necessary», the FL1 statement, «Dr [name], [degree]». |
| Medicare | «This offer is not available to Medicare or Medicaid beneficiaries.» (when the clinic bills them) |
| Reviews line | «[number] Google reviews» only if true and every patient was asked. |

## 8. Not verified

- Texas 22 TAC 75.5 (prepaid plans) amendment and the definitions behind s.101.201.
- California regulations after the November 2021 compilation; Florida discipline grounds (s.460.413) and solicitation laws; New York chiropractic-specific provisions.
- The Medicare statute's exceptions and penalty amounts: give none.
